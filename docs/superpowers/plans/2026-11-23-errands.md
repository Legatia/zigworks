# harness: errands and upkeep (sections 3 and 4 of the household duties) — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** the butler keeps the household's errands and upkeep. **Lists:** shared lists (`shopping` by default) in `state/lists/`, added to and taken from by message by the owner and the household's members ("add milk", "we're out of coffee"), `/list`, `/list clear`, posted on request and before a planned shop (a calendar event, or a weekly time). **Reorders:** a shop's MCP server prices the list; the whole order (each line, the total, the delivery) is proposed to the owner, and only the owner's tap orders it, through the gate's money check and caps. **Deliveries:** tracking numbers and carriers from the owner's mail and messages in `state/deliveries`; the carrier's page read on a schedule; "out for delivery", "waiting at the locker" and "delivered" lines; a hint for the doorbell. **Upkeep:** `state/upkeep` holds things with an interval (by time, or by the car's distance from carcloud's odometer) and documents with an end date; a line a week before and one on the day with [Done] (or [Renewed]); "the boiler was serviced today" in chat does the same; `/upkeep`; `/setup upkeep` offers the usual ones.

**Architecture:** the pure parts in new files: `lists.zig` (list files, the words harness reads in a message, the shop's quote and order, the proposal's words, `state/errands`), `deliveries.zig` (carriers, tracking numbers in text, a page's status, `state/deliveries`, lines, the hint), `upkeep.zig` (items, intervals, documents, the odometer, due lines, the words of "done", the usual ones). `core.zig` gets one field (`errands`), a few hook lines (open, close, tick, the owner's and members' text, commands, an item settled, `/setup`), one block `const Errands = struct { … }` at the end of the code and one block of tests at the end of the file. `fake_shop.zig` is the shop's stand-in (`harness-fake --mcp --shop`); `fake_mcp.zig`'s car reads an `odometer` file when there is one.

**Tech Stack:** Zig 0.17.1, std only; macOS, Linux, Windows.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-harness-butler-household-design.md` §3, §4, Tests; the butler spec's UX rules and red lines; the email spec (mail is data; `mail_list`/`mail_read`, `role = mine`); the ledger's household (members' limits), money gate and caps, quiet hours, routines and `/setup`, carcloud's `car_status` odometer.

## Review Focus: never buying unasked, and members' limits

1. **Only the owner's tap buys.** A reorder is proposed as an inbox item (kind `confirm`, waiter `errand order`) in main; the call it would make is kept in `state/errands` under the item's id, and only an `answered` close with key `order` made by the owner's button (or `/answer`) starts it. Expiry, Not now, a void or withdrawn item, a member's press (members' presses are not the owner's), a restart, a schedule: nothing is ordered. The tap makes exactly the kept call, once: the call is taken off the store before it is made; a second press finds nothing. (`a_reorder_is_bought_only_on_the_owners_tap`, `not_now_and_expiry_order_nothing`, `a_tap_after_a_restart_still_orders_once`.)
2. **Through the money gate.** The order tool must be a money tool in mcp.ini with an amount field (else nothing is proposed, and nothing is called); the quote tool must not be one. The call goes through `agent.stepCall` as the owner's, the gate deciding it again (a `deny` stops it; `/dryrun on` makes nothing; the caps of mcp.ini and `[money]` checked before the asker), with an asker that says yes to that exact canonical call once and no to anything else. A quote over the per-call cap is said in a line with no button. (`the_order_goes_through_the_gates_caps_and_dry_run`, `a_shop_whose_order_tool_is_not_money_is_refused`.)
3. **Members add, remove and read lists; nothing else.** Their list words are read by harness itself (no model run); they can't clear a list, set a post or a shop, see deliveries or upkeep, or answer an order. A member's add that brings the list to its size makes a proposal to the owner, never to the member. (`a_member_adds_milk_in_the_group`, `members_cannot_clear_or_order`.)
4. **Mail and pages are data.** Tracking numbers are found by harness's code, never by a model; a mail's text is not logged or kept (only the number, the carrier, the sender's name and the expected day); a carrier's page is read from harness's own table of addresses (never a link from a mail), with a checked number, and only its status is kept.

## Rulings

(Each: what – why – cost if wrong.)

1. **List words are read by harness, not by a model.** What: "add X [to the/my/our [NAME] list | to NAME]", "put X on the [NAME] list", "we're / we are / we're all out of X", "remove / delete / take off / cross off X [from the list]", "got X" and "bought X" (only when X is on a list), and to read one "list", "the list", "shopping list", "what's on the list", "show the list"; Polish "dodaj X", "usuń X", "lista", "lista zakupów", "co jest na liście". Several items by commas and "and"/"i". Why: instant, the same for members, no model cost, and a member's words never reach a model that holds tools. Cost if wrong: another phrasing goes to the model, which has no list tool and says it can't.
2. **A bare "add X" is guarded.** What: it counts when X is at most 6 words and 60 bytes, has no "?", no time (`9:30`), no " to " (but for a list), and none of the words reminder, meeting, event, appointment, calendar, alarm, timer, routine, trigger, task, workflow, member, rule, note, contact, birthday, file, folder, topic, brief, light, lights, heating. Why: "add a reminder for 9" and "add Tomek to the meeting" are for the model. Cost if wrong: "add a new light bulb" goes to the model.
3. **Members: add, remove, read; the owner alone clears and sets up.** What: a member's list words act at once and are answered in their place (their chat or the group); `/list` (reading) works for them; `/list clear`, `/list … every`, `/setup reorder`, deliveries and upkeep are the owner's. The owner isn't told of members' adds. Why: the ledger's household rights ("they may add to lists"), quiet by default. Cost if wrong: a member who did the shop can't clear the list in one go (they take items off).
4. **List files.** What: `state/lists/NAME`, a line an item (`#` lines comments), written whole; names a-z, 0-9, `-`, `_` up to 32; at most 20 lists, 200 items, 80 bytes an item; an item already there (case aside) isn't added twice; "to the hardware list" makes the list `hardware`. Why: the owner can read and edit them. Cost if wrong: none found.
5. **A planned shop.** What: a calendar event (calmcp's, as the diary reads it) whose title has shop, shopping, groceries, supermarket, market, zakupy, sklep, a shop's name or a list's name gets its list 60 minutes before it starts, once; `/list [NAME] every DAY HH:MM` posts it then each week; neither when the list is empty. Both as `schedule:lists` (quiet hours and busy events hold them). Why: "before a planned shop (a calendar event or a routine)". Cost if wrong: a shop with another title gets no list (`/list every`, or ask).
6. **The shop's contract.** What: a shop is an MCP server in mcp.ini with a tool that prices a basket (its name has quote, price, basket or cart; not a money tool) and one money tool (the order) with `amount =` naming a field. The quote gets `{"items": ["milk", …]}` and answers JSON `{"total": 64.2, "currency": "PLN", "lines": [{"item": "milk", "product": "Mleko 2% 1 l", "price": 4.2}], "missing": ["oat milk"], "delivery": "Sat 28 Nov, 10:00–12:00"}`; the order gets `{"items": [the found ones], AMOUNT: total}` (the total a ceiling the shop holds to) and answers in words. Why: no shop has a standard; the total in the call is what the caps check. Cost if wrong: a real shop's server needs a small adapter.
7. **When a reorder is proposed.** What: `/setup reorder SHOP [LIST] [at N items] [every DAY HH:MM]` (default 10 items, `shopping`); when an add takes the list from under N to N, or at the weekly time with something on it; one open proposal per shop; after Not now (or no answer) the size proposes again only after the list fell under it. Why: "when a list reaches its size or a date", without nagging. Cost if wrong: a declined order waits for the date.
8. **The proposal is the whole order.** What: one message, "Order from frisco: 64.20 PLN, delivery Sat 28 Nov, 10:00–12:00." then a line per item with the product and price, then "Not found: oat milk (stays on the list).", [Order it] [Not now], expiring after `approval_seconds`; by `schedule:reorder` (quiet hours hold it). Why: UX rules 1 and 3; "proposed in full". Cost if wrong: a long basket takes two messages.
9. **The tap makes the call as the owner's.** What: `by = owner`, label `reorder`, the built-in main definition (the owner's `agents/main.md` tools don't narrow it), an asker that allows that exact canonical call once; the shop's words read back; the ordered items taken off the list (the missing stay); a failed call takes nothing off. Why: the owner's tap is the owner's request; scheduled-money rules are for runs nobody watches. Cost if wrong: none found; a `deny mcp SHOP/*` rule still stops it.
10. **Caps before asking.** What: a total over the shop's (or `[money]`'s) per-call cap is said in a line with no button; the per-day cap is the gate's at the tap (its reason read back). Why: never ask for what the gate will refuse. Cost if wrong: a day-cap refusal comes after the tap.
11. **Tracking numbers.** What: found in a mail's subject and text and in the owner's messages: UPS `1Z…`, DHL `JJD…`, postal `AA123456789PL`, DPD `…U`, and runs of 10–30 digits next to a tracking word (tracking, parcel, numer przesyłki, …) or a carrier's name; the carrier from the shape, the mail's sender, or the one carrier the text names. The owner's message must name a carrier or say track/parcel/package/paczka/przesyłka; a number with no carrier is asked about, not kept. Why: an order number or a phone isn't a parcel. Cost if wrong: a carrier's odd format is missed (the owner can say it).
12. **Mail is read by harness itself.** What: with your mailbox on (`/setup mail mine`), every 30 minutes `mail_list` `{"mailbox", "kind": "delivery", "since": "14d"}`, then `mail_read` of each new one; kept: the number, the carrier, the sender's name, mailmcp's expected day; the ids seen in `state/deliveries`. A parcel found in mail gets no line for what its first read finds. Why: the diary's and the mail conversation's way (harness's own reads of servers set up for it); no line for an old parcel. Cost if wrong: a parcel already out for delivery when its mail is first read gets no out-for-delivery line (the delivered one comes).
13. **Carrier pages.** What: harness's table (InPost's public tracking API, DPD Poland, GLS, DHL, UPS, Poczta Polska, FedEx), the number checked (letters and digits, up to 40) and put in the address; read by harness itself, a `deny fetch` rule on the address stops it; every 2 hours from 08:00 to 20:00, every 30 minutes once out for delivery; delivered parcels dropped after 2 days, others after 30. Only the status is kept. Pages that need a browser to show it say nothing (the mail's day still feeds the hint). Why: a fixed address can't be steered by a mail. Cost if wrong: DHL, UPS, Poczta Polska and FedEx pages (scripts) give no lines.
14. **Delivery lines.** What: "Your DPD parcel from Zalando is out for delivery.", "… is waiting at the parcel locker.", "… was delivered.", each once, to main as `schedule:deliveries` (quiet hours hold them). Why: UX rules 1 and 2. Cost if wrong: none found.
15. **The doorbell's hint.** What: `Core.deliveryHint(a) ?[]const u8`: "probably the DPD parcel" (out for delivery, or due today by its mail, not delivered or at a locker), "probably the DPD or the InPost parcel" for two, "probably a parcel" for more or none named. Why: the door builder's "probably the DHL parcel". Cost if wrong: none found.
16. **Upkeep's file.** What: `state/upkeep` (JSON, written whole): items with months or days, km, the last time done (day and km), documents with an end day and their renewal (insurance and inspection 12 months; warranties none), and the car's readings (first and last). Why: one file, read and written whole. Cost if wrong: none found.
17. **Upkeep's lines.** What: from 09:00 once a day (the owner's time) as `schedule:upkeep` (quiet hours hold them): "Boiler service is due in a week (Wed 2 Dec)." and "Boiler service is due today.", each once per due day; an item first seen late says "Boiler service was due on 12 Mar." once; documents "Car insurance ends in a week (Fri 15 Jan)." / "… ends today."; each with [Done] (documents that renew: [Renewed]; warranties: no button), open for 7 days. [Done] counts the next from that day (and the car's distance from its last reading); [Renewed] moves the end a renewal on. Why: the spec's two lines and [Done]. Cost if wrong: an item done but not said stays due in `/upkeep`.
18. **The car's distance.** What: with a `km` item and carcloud in mcp.ini as `car`, harness reads `car_status` once a day (its own read, like the diary's) for "odometer: N km" (miles turned to km); due at the last service's km plus the interval; "a week before" is when the car is within a week of driving of it, the rate from readings at least 7 days apart (else 40 km a day). The line names both numbers. Why: carcloud's odometer; the spec's "by distance or date". Cost if wrong: a car that drives in bursts hears it early or late.
19. **"Serviced today" in chat.** What: the owner's message names an item (a word of its name, as said: boiler, filters, smoke alarm, car) with a done word (serviced, replaced, changed, cleaned, tested, checked, done, renewed, inspected) and today, yesterday, a date or no day (today); a question or a future ("will", "tomorrow", "next") is not; owner only. Answer: "Boiler service done today; next due 23 Nov 2027." Why: the spec. Cost if wrong: an unusual phrasing goes to the model (which says to use /upkeep done).
20. **The usual ones.** What: `/setup upkeep` offers, one at a time with [Add it] [Not now] and a reply with the last date: boiler service yearly, filters every 3 months, smoke alarm test monthly; with a car: the car's service every 15,000 km or yearly, its insurance and its inspection (a reply with the end date). `/setup` says how many are kept. Why: "setup offers the usual ones", one by one as the routines are. Cost if wrong: none found.
21. **One errands thread.** What: the reads that wait on a server (the quote, mail, pages, the odometer) run on one thread of their own, started by the tick; close waits for it. Why: the tick never blocks. Cost if wrong: a slow shop delays the delivery reads a little.
22. **Tests point carriers at the stand-in.** What: `Errands.track_url` (empty in use) replaces the table's addresses with `URLCARRIER-NUMBER`. Why: no network in tests. Cost if wrong: none.

## Done

Built on platform/errands (from 4e362de), test-first: each new test seen failing (stubs or a missing hook), then the code, then the whole suite, then a commit. No existing test was changed.

- Tasks 1–7 as above; Task 8: six smoke checks (a member's "add milk, eggs", `/setup reorder`, the proposal at the list's size, the tap ordering once, a parcel named in a message, "serviced today"), README "Errands and upkeep" (and the Household, Commands and intro lines), TESTERS.md days 11 and 12, what to look for, "never buys without your tap".
- Found while building (each a test first):
  - The gate turns fetch's default `ask` into "no one to ask" for a scheduled who: a carrier's page is decided as if someone could be asked, so only a `deny` rule stops it (Ruling 13; `a_deny_rule_stops_a_carriers_page`).
  - A page read whose status isn't recognised says "on its way", not "not read yet".
  - The name of an upkeep item ends at the first " every " or " ends " ("Home insurance ends 1.12.2026 renews every 6 months").
  - `/reload` waits while the errands' job runs (it reads `mcp_tools`), and the job starts under the settings lock, so neither slips past the other.
- Who values of the errands' own jobs, one constant each in `Errands` (core.zig): `by_lists` = `schedule:lists`, `by_reorder` = `schedule:reorder`, `by_deliveries` = `schedule:deliveries`, `by_upkeep` = `schedule:upkeep`. No rules sections are shipped for them. (The coordinator's planned `harness:NAME` kind replaces these.)
- The doorbell's hint for the door builder: `Core.deliveryHint(a) ?[]const u8`.
- Left out: ordering through a shop's website with a login (`web`); speech in a room adding to lists (it goes to the model, which has no list tool); a list tool for the model; carriers' pages that need a browser (DHL, UPS, Poczta Polska, FedEx give no status); a smoke check of a carrier's page (the table has no setting to point at the stand-in; unit tests cover it); nothing run against a real shop, carrier, mailbox or car.
