# harness as Home Assistant's voice agent, and homemcp's `announce` (Part 9 of the butler spec) — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** People speak to Home Assistant's voice satellites and harness answers, in the language spoken (Polish included); the house's speakers say urgent things. Harness serves an OpenAI-compatible `POST /v1/chat/completions` (and `GET /v1/models`) that Home Assistant's llama.cpp integration (2026.8) calls as its conversation agent: on `[voice_agent] listen` (127.0.0.1:8765 by default), a bearer token stored as a secret (`harness secret add ha-agent`), the body capped. What was said runs as a turn in a conversation `home`, by `room:NAME` (anyone in that room may speak), with the household's rights; a guarded call becomes the owner's one-tap question in Telegram and the spoken answer says so in one sentence. homemcp gets an `announce` tool (Home Assistant's `assist_satellite.announce`, or `tts.speak` for media players): allowlisted per speaker, text only, rate-limited, read back; harness's triggers say urgent things with it (`announce =`), and only urgent ones at night.

**Architecture:** `who.zig` gets the kind `room` and `isHousehold` (member or room); `gate.zig`'s member order decides a room's call the same way with `[room]`/`[room:NAME]` sections; `agent.zig` offers a room's run the household's tools and refuses an `announce` call in quiet hours for runs nobody watches unless they are urgent (`Run.hush`). New `speech.zig`: what Home Assistant's request says (room, language, the words), what is spoken back (made for the ear), harness's own lines in Polish and English, the speech contexts. New `voiceagent.zig`: the listener (two routes, the token, the caps, JSON or server-sent events). `core.zig` gets one block: the speech turn (beside the chat queue, one at a time, its own client, counted as a background run), its asker (the owner's item, waiter `room NAME`, `state/room-asks`), the owner's yes making the call, start/stop/reload, `/status`, triggers.ini's `announce` action and the quiet-hours check. homemcp: kind `satellite`, action `announce`, `[home] tts`, the tool, `home://speakers`, `check`'s speakers line, discover, homemcp-sim recording announcements.

**Tech Stack:** Zig 0.17.1, std only; macOS, Linux, Windows.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-harness-butler-design.md` (UX rules, Part 9, red lines); research `harness/research/smart-home-voice.md`; the ledger's conversations, members (`[member]` rules, the owner's one-tap question), the inbox, quiet hours and `urgent`, providers, homemcp's resources and guarded tool.

## Review Focus: who may speak, and what they may do

What a reviewer checks first, each with the test that holds it:

1. **Anyone in a room is nobody in particular.** A speech run is `room:NAME`, never `owner` or `member:NAME`; `[member]` lines don't reach it and `[room]` lines don't reach members; the shared part and `[agent:…]` sections only deny or ask for it; no grant covers it; no "Allow 24 h". (`room_is_a_who_kind`, `a_rooms_defaults`, `room_and_member_sections_stay_apart`.)
2. **Speech does only the household's things.** Alone: search, homemcp's reads and ordinary devices. The owner's one-tap question: other MCP tools, money and guarded calls (locks, the garage, the alarm, valves). Never: commands, files, memory, history, `web`, skills, delegate, ask_owner, setup kinds, `send`. Its run sees nothing of the owner's (no `agents/main.md` body, memory, summaries, history, other conversations). (`a_rooms_defaults`, `speech_sees_nothing_of_the_owners`.)
3. **Nothing guarded is approved by voice.** The owner's tap in Telegram makes the exact call, decided by the gate again; a "yes" said in the room is only words to the model. (`a_spoken_unlock_becomes_the_owners_question`.)
4. **Only Home Assistant gets in.** Two routes; the token compared in constant time before the body is read; 401 without it; 413 over 256 KB; a listener only where config says; the token never logged. (`voice_agent_refuses_without_the_token`, `voice_agent_answers_two_routes_only`, `voice_agent_binds_what_config_says`.)
5. **The house speaks at night only for urgent things.** (`announce_is_quiet_at_night_unless_urgent`.)

## Rulings

(Each: what – why – cost if wrong. Added to as they are made.)

1. **Speech is `room:NAME`, its rules `[room]` and `[room:NAME]`.** What: NAME is Home Assistant's area of the satellite that heard it, lower case, spaces as `_`, other marks dropped (`room:living_room`, `room:łazienka`); `room:unknown` when no area comes. Why: what reaches harness is a place, not a person: Home Assistant has no speaker ID, so a guest, a child, the TV or someone at an open window speak alike; a room claims no identity, and the owner's rules read as they think ("nothing from the kids' room"); a member is a person proven by their own Telegram account, and `[member]` lines were written for known people. Cost if wrong: a member's own voice gets only the room's rights (without speaker ID, by design).
2. **Speech has the household's rights, decided by the members' order.** What: search; homemcp's `home_state`, `home_wait`, `home_set` alone; every other MCP tool and money (`home_guarded`) the owner's one-tap question; `fetch`/`browse` only with a `[room]` allow line; never commands, files, memory, history, `web`, skills, delegate, ask_owner, setup kinds; no `send` (the answer is spoken). Why: the spec's household-member rights; one decision path, already reviewed. Cost if wrong: an owner who wants less from speech writes `[room] ask mcp */home_set *`.
3. **The room and the language come from lines the owner pastes into the integration's Instructions, or Home Assistant's own area line.** What: `Room: {{ area_name(llm_context.device_id) }}` and `Language: {{ llm_context.language }}`; with Home Assistant's AI tools on, its "You are in area X" line too; nothing else of its system message reaches the model. Words that aren't plain (letters, Polish ones too, digits, spaces, `-`, `_`, `'`; 48 bytes) are no room. Why: the llama.cpp route sends no device, satellite or area (only `user`, its conversation id: `llama_cpp/entity.py`); its Instructions are a template that has `llm_context` (`conversation/chat_log.py`); Home Assistant's prompt (and its exposed entities with AI tools on) would slow every answer and is not harness's to follow. Cost if wrong: without the lines every room is `room:unknown` and `[room:NAME]` sections don't apply.
4. **Speech turns run beside the chat queue, one at a time.** What: on the request's thread, with their own model client and context, counted as a background run (`/reload` waits, `/stop` stops it); a second request waits for the first within its own time. Why: a speaker waiting behind the owner's long research turn would time out. Cost if wrong: two rooms speaking in the same second: the second answer comes seconds later.
5. **20 seconds a spoken answer (`[voice_agent] seconds`, 5–60).** What: at the limit the run stops and harness says one short line; never a 5xx, 408 or 429 once a turn began. Why: Home Assistant sets no limit on the agent's call (the llama.cpp integration keeps the OpenAI client's default, 600 s, and retries a timeout, 408, 409, 429 or 5xx twice; the pipeline has none on the conversation stage; only the model list has 10 s); the person at the speaker is the limit; 20 s covers two or three model calls and homemcp's read-back (up to 5 s), far inside 600 s; a retried request would run the turn twice. Cost if wrong: a slow model or a long search is cut at 20 s.
6. **A guarded call by voice becomes the owner's one-tap question; the spoken answer says so.** What: "Someone in Kitchen asked by voice to unlock the front door." [Unlock] [No], and that harness can't tell who spoke; the call stops there (deferred) and the model is told to say in one short sentence that the owner was asked (harness says it when the model says nothing); the owner's Yes makes that exact call as `room:NAME` through the gate again and adds what the device said to the owner's message; nothing is said in the room afterwards. Kept in `state/room-asks`; at most 5 waiting per room; the same call asked again is the same item. Why: never approved by voice in a room; a turn can't wait ten minutes. Cost if wrong: the person in the room doesn't hear the outcome.
7. **What is spoken is made for the ear by harness.** What: markdown marks, links and emoji dropped, lines joined, cut at a sentence's end to 400 bytes; harness's own lines (too long, busy, an error, asked) in Polish or English (the `Language:` line, else Polish letters in what was said), else English. Why: Home Assistant reads the text aloud as it comes. Cost if wrong: a German household hears harness's few own lines in English.
8. **A context per Home Assistant conversation, in memory, five minutes, at most eight.** What: keyed by the request's `user` (Home Assistant's conversation id); never the owner's contexts; never read for memory; speech runs are not summarised. Why: Home Assistant keeps a satellite's follow-ups ("dimmer") under one id for five minutes. Cost if wrong: after a restart a follow-up starts fresh.
9. **Two routes, the token first.** What: `GET /v1/models` answers one model, `harness`; `POST /v1/chat/completions` answers in JSON or, with `stream: true`, server-sent events (one chunk, a stop chunk, `[DONE]`); anything else 404 (405 for another method on these); the bearer token compared as SHA-256 digests in constant time before the body is read, missing or wrong 401 (logged once a minute); a body over 256 KB 413, unread; head and body within 10 s; at most 4 connections; Home Assistant's `tools`, `max_tokens`, `temperature`, `top_p` and `model` ignored. Why: what the integration sends (models at setup with 10 s, a short test chat with a dummy tool, then chats, `stream` as its setting says). Cost if wrong: with Home Assistant's AI tools on and hundreds of exposed entities a request may pass 256 KB (README: turn them off).
10. **The listener binds exactly `[voice_agent] listen`.** What: 127.0.0.1:8765 by default; a LAN address only when written; an IP literal only, `0.0.0.0`, `::` and IPv4 addresses written as IPv6 (`[::ffff:0.0.0.0]`) refused, `off` switches it off; with `harness run` only, and only while the token (`[voice_agent] token`, the secret `ha-agent`) of at least 16 characters is stored; `/reload` applies changes. Why: the house's network, not every network the machine is on. Cost if wrong: an owner whose LAN address changes (DHCP) reserves it in the router or updates `listen`.
11. **Only urgent things speak at night.** What: a call to an MCP tool named `announce` (homemcp's, on any server) from a run nobody watches (a schedule, a trigger, a workflow) in quiet hours is refused before the gate unless its trigger or workflow is urgent; the owner's own runs, speech and commands run by hand speak whenever. triggers.ini's `announce = TEXT` says a line per event (filled like `say`, in the event's room when it has one) on the house's speakers, allowed as listed by the owner (a deny rule stops it). Why: the spec's "within quiet hours' rules"; one check at the call covers `do` runs, workflow tool steps and the trigger key. Cost if wrong: another server's tool called `announce` is quiet at night too.
12. **Speakers that may announce are allowlisted in the home file.** What: kind `satellite` (an Assist satellite, `assist_satellite.*`, said through `assist_satellite.announce`) and `media` (through `tts.speak` with `[home] tts = tts.NAME`), each with `announce` on its `allow` line; Home Assistant only. discover writes satellites with `allow = announce`, media players without it. Why: a satellite is there to speak; a media player may be the living room's music. Cost if wrong: the owner adds `announce` to a media player by hand.
13. **`announce` is text only, rate-limited and read back.** What: `message` 1–300 bytes, no control characters, `<` or `>` (no markup, no sound files); `speaker` (a name or alias) or `room` (its speakers, else every speaker that may announce); neither: every one. At most one announcement a speaker every 10 s and 20 an hour (in memory). A satellite's call returns once Home Assistant played it (its REST call waits up to 10 s) and its state is read; a media player must show `playing` within 5 s; else "sent, but … has not confirmed". Logged like actions. Not guarded (it changes nothing that stays). `home://speakers` and `check`'s "Speakers:" line list them. Why: a message, not a stream; a loop can't keep the house talking. Cost if wrong: two urgent events within 10 s on one speaker: the second is refused (said in its answer).

14. **Only the model's last message is spoken.** What: its text before a tool call ("Let me check.") is never said; an empty last message gets harness's own line (that the owner was asked, or no answer). Why: a spoken answer is one thing said; the model's earlier text is it working. Cost if wrong: none found.
15. **`home` is a name in the log, not a place.** What: speech's conversation is in no state/conversations line and posts nowhere; the owner's question goes to `main`; a Telegram topic or group may also be called `home` and shares only the name in the log (contexts keyed apart, speech never summarised, the owner's commands in a group not read). Why: a post to a `home` that isn't a conversation would open a topic of that name in the owner's chat. Cost if wrong: `harness log runs` lists both under `home`.
16. **triggers.ini's `announce` is for event triggers, said beside the action, three events at most, each once.** What: not on schedules or briefs (a routine at a set time says it from a workflow's tool step or a `do` run); on a thread of its own, so the post and its one-tap button never wait for the speakers (after the review: said first, a house of satellites held the leak's post up to a minute and a half); a burst of events says three; an event said is remembered (the last 256, in memory), so a retry over pending events says nothing again; a trigger can't poll `announce` as its source (it would speak every poll, at night too). Why: an event is what happens in the house; a schedule's lines belong to the routines. Cost if wrong: "say goodnight at 22:00" needs a workflow step; after a restart an event retried is said again once.
17. **The token is the owner's to make.** What: `harness secret add ha-agent` stores any value of 16 characters or more (the README suggests `openssl rand -hex 24`); harness doesn't generate one. Why: `secret add` is main.zig's, another builder's part now; the same value goes into Home Assistant's API key field. Cost if wrong: one more step for the owner (guided setup can make it later).

18. **Speech waits on no device** (after the review). What: `home_wait` is not among speech's defaults (members keep it); `[room] allow mcp */home_wait *` lifts it. Why: an answer is spoken within seconds, and homemcp serves one call at a time: a wait of up to 300 s asked by anyone in a room held every other homemcp call (a leak's announcement, the owner's valve). Cost if wrong: "tell me when the door closes" gets a no by voice.
19. **The listener after the review.** What: 16 connections at once (not 4: a device on the network holding a few open kept Home Assistant out); a stop lets an answer being made be written (a cut connection would have Home Assistant send the request again, and a turn run twice); its accept polls its stop flag. Why: availability, and a turn made once. Cost if wrong: none found.
20. **A room's section must be written as its who** (after the review). What: `[room:Kitchen]`, `[room:Łazienka]`, `[room:kid's_room]` are refused in `rules`, naming how to write them. Why: they would never match, and a deny the owner wrote there would silently not apply. Cost if wrong: none found.
21. **homemcp's `announce` after the review.** What: a room matches in any case, Latin letters too (a Polish room mismatched played in the whole house); a media player playing already is "sent, but … can't be read back"; the limits count on the monotonic clock; line and paragraph separators, direction and invisible marks are control characters too; speech's answers in a language without spaces are cut between letters, never to nothing. Cost if wrong: none found.
